UrbanCart Data Dictionary and Hypothesis Coverage Review

Review date: 2026-05-06

Overview
Three raw datasets are available for the EDA project:
- `customers.csv`
- `orders.csv`
- `deliveries.csv`

Observed dataset sizes
- `customers.csv`: 5,000 rows
- `orders.csv`: 73,022 rows
- `deliveries.csv`: 73,022 rows

Join structure
- `customers.customer_id` joins to `orders.customer_id`
- `orders.order_id` joins to `deliveries.order_id`
- All orders currently have a matching delivery record, and all delivery records have a matching order record

Data quality snapshot
- No missing values were detected in any column across the three files
- `customer_id`, `order_id`, and `delivery_id` appear to be unique identifiers
- Date coverage observed:
  - Customer signup dates: 2024-01-01 to 2026-03-31
  - Order dates: 2024-01-02 to 2026-04-14

2. Can These Datasets Test the Defined Hypotheses?

Hypothesis 1
Statement: Customers with more reliable delivery experiences are more likely to be retained.
Assessment: Mostly testable with the current data.
Why:
- Delivery reliability is represented by `delivery_status`, `delay_minutes`, `actual_time_min`, `promised_time_min`, `order_accuracy`, and `customer_rating`
- Retention or repeat behavior can be derived from future ordering activity using `order_date` and `customer_id`
What is needed analytically:
- A clear retention definition, such as whether a customer places another order within 30, 60, or 90 days after a focal order

Hypothesis 2
Statement: Customers who receive more personalized or promotion-driven engagement are more likely to place repeat orders and remain active.
Assessment: Partially testable.
Why:
- Promotion-driven behavior can be tested using `promo_used` and `discount_amount_usd`
- Repeat behavior can be derived from order history
Gap:
- There is no direct data on personalization, such as recommendations shown, campaign targeting, email or push notification exposure, loyalty messaging, or individualized offers
Conclusion:
- The promotion part of the hypothesis is testable, but the personalization part is not supported by the available datasets

Hypothesis 3
Statement: Customers who use UrbanCart across multiple vendor categories or service types are more likely to be retained.
Assessment: Testable with the current data.
Why:
- `vendor_category` and `delivery_type` allow customer-level breadth-of-usage features to be created
- Retention or repeat behavior can be derived from order dates and customer IDs
What is needed analytically:
- Customer-level features such as number of distinct categories used or number of distinct delivery types used before a retention evaluation window

3. Data Gaps and Limitations

Gap 1: No explicit retention or churn label
Impact:
- Retention is not directly stored as a field and must be defined from repeat ordering behavior
- Results may change depending on the time window selected
Recommendation:
- Create a formal retention definition before analysis, for example active again within 30, 60, or 90 days

Gap 2: No direct personalization or CRM interaction data
Impact:
- The analysis cannot fully test whether personalized interactions drive retention
- Only generic promotion usage can be evaluated
Examples of missing fields:
- Campaign exposure
- Push notification history
- Email engagement
- Recommendation impressions or clicks
- Loyalty program participation

Gap 3: Limited customer experience context beyond delivery outcomes
Impact:
- Important churn drivers may be missing, such as cancellations, refunds, app issues, support tickets, stockouts, or payment failures
- Hypothesis 1 can be tested for delivery quality, but not for the full service experience

Gap 4: Right-censoring risk near the end of the date range
Impact:
- Customers who ordered near 2026-04-14 may appear not retained simply because there is little future observation time
- Newer customers may be unfairly labeled as inactive if the retention window is too long
Recommendation:
- Use cohort cutoffs or exclude very recent orders/customers when calculating retention outcomes

Gap 5: No direct profitability or acquisition-cost fields
Impact:
- The business context emphasizes profitability and retention economics, but this dataset cannot directly measure customer lifetime value, CAC, or margin

4. Overall Sufficiency Verdict

The current datasets are sufficient to begin EDA and to test two of the three hypotheses well enough for an initial project:
- Hypothesis 1: testable
- Hypothesis 3: testable

Hypothesis 2 is only partially testable:
- Promotion effects: testable
- Personalization effects: not testable with current data

Overall, the data is strong enough for a solid retention-focused EDA if retention is carefully defined and the missing personalization fields are acknowledged as a limitation.
